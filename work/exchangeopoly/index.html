Case study · University of Edinburgh, Design Informatics · Peoplehood project · 2022–24

Designing a research instrument to study collective decision-making

Group decisions are hard to observe: people say one thing in interviews and do another together. Exchangeopoly is a board game I developed so that decisions about governance and value exchange play out at the table, where they can be studied.

My role
Postdoctoral Research Associate. I led the co-design research and developed the method
Type
  • Generative
  • New method
Partners
Blackwood Group, Timebanking UK
Context
Peoplehood: Blockchain for Social Good

How do communities decide together what to share, what it's worth, and who gets a say?

The question

Peoplehood explored how digital tools, including blockchain, could support community exchange for people facing social isolation. The team needed evidence about how these communities actually govern themselves and exchange value, and an approach that could include people whom standard research methods tend to miss.

My role

I led co-design research with marginalised communities facing social isolation in several Scottish cities. I developed Exchangeopoly as a research tool. I also managed research operations and delivery, and supervised the junior designers and PhD students on the team.

Methods

  • Co-design sessions with users and stakeholders
  • Game-based elicitation
  • Inclusive & accessible methods
  • Facilitated workshops
  • Observation
  • Thematic analysis

I designed and used a variety of methods to surface needs and barriers that standard approaches would have missed. The game builds on my earlier participatory game work (Appetite, 2014), where I found that game rules lead people to act out real-world dynamics rather than describe them.

Who I worked with

The University of Edinburgh Design Informatics team; partners Blackwood Group (a housing and care provider) and Timebanking UK; and community members across Scotland.
