About

I study how people make, share and decide together

I'm a mixed-methods user researcher with a PhD in Human-Computer Interaction and 9+ years leading end-to-end research in complex, multidisciplinary settings. I'm happiest with an open, messy question: why people do what they do, and what a product, service or policy should do about it.

At Samsung R&D I did foundational and evaluative research for the Samsung Z1 and Tizen OS, Bixby, Samsung Health and wearables. I ran the in-home usability research behind Inito's fertility monitor. With the Centre for Knowledge Societies and the Gates Foundation I led field research in India, Kenya and Indonesia. I co-founded CookUp, a 0→1 platform for indie chefs. Since my PhD at Northumbria University I've worked as a postdoc at the University of Edinburgh (Peoplehood) and now at City St George's, University of London and UAL (MoSaIC, with DEFRA, Arup and UKRI).

How I work

  • Method follows the question. I use contextual inquiry, interviews, diary studies, usability testing, surveys, A/B and unmoderated studies, co-design sessions and future-scenario research, often several in one study.
  • I build research instruments when off-the-shelf ones don't work. Examples are games, probes and devices that make behaviour observable rather than just reported (Exchangeopoly, Soil Stories).
  • Ethics isn't a checkbox. At Northumbria I led a trauma-informed study with BAME participants navigating discrimination and institutional failure. I designed the safety, consent and data-handling protocols, and the recommendations we co-created with participants fed into the university's equity policy.
  • Insight only matters if it moves a decision. I bring stakeholders in early and write findings in the team's language, from sprint teams to Samsung leadership in Korea and the US, and from WHO to DEFRA.
  • I build research capability. I mentor junior designers and PhD students, run research operations (recruitment, screeners, agencies, GDPR), and embed user-centred thinking in the teams I join.

What makes my lens different

My PhD thesis, Designing for Participatory Visioning: HCI for Reconciling Sustainability Visions with Everyday Practice, and my postdoctoral work draw on more-than-human design and feminist science and technology studies. In practice, that means I design for ecological and community outcomes, not only for the individual in front of a screen. It's a useful counterweight when a product affects households, creators' audiences or the places people live.

In the research community

I'm an Associate Chair for ACM DIS (2024–26) and review for CHI, CSCW, DIS, PDC and TEI. I won a TEI Best Paper Presentation award in 2018.

Before research

I trained in communication design (Nottingham Trent University) and new media design (NID, Ahmedabad), and started out in advertising. So I'm comfortable prototyping, visualising findings and telling the story in a room.
