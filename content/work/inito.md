## The context

Before production launch, the team needed to know where people struggled to set up and use the device at home, and what had to change so that first-time users could complete onboarding successfully. They also needed to understand why people stopped using fertility monitoring at home.

## My role

I led the research from ideation to implementation, testing the product before launch. I scoped and designed the studies, and turned the findings into redesign recommendations for production.

## Methods

I ran in-home usability sessions with 18 women across their ovulation cycle, in their own homes. That's the real context of use: a fertility hormone test is private, often done at a specific time of day, and done without help.

## Who I worked with

I was the only researcher on a team of 14 engineers and programmers, and I worked directly with the startup's founders to implement product changes.

## Key insights

> Social stigma around fertility monitoring made women reluctant to use the device openly at home, a sensitive barrier to adoption.

App notifications could expose fertility information on shared or visible phones. This informed recommendations for discreet, user-controlled alerts.

## Impact

- Delivered redesign recommendations for the production launch through mixed-methods evaluations.
- The redesigned onboarding flows raised task completion from **38% to 89%**.
