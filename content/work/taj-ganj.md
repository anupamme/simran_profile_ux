## The question

The Taj Mahal's three entry gates, East, West and South, sit right on the edge of Taj Ganj, and tourist traffic through them was irregular and unbalanced. How could tourist flow be regularised and visitor experiences made more inclusive, without disrupting the community that lives there?

## My role

I was part of a team of field researchers from the National Institute of Design. My area of study was the residential area leading to the West Gate, one of the gates that attracts heavy tourist traffic.

## Methods

I followed the classic phenomenological approach of ethnography: engaging with the community, building rapport, gaining access to the site and its gatekeepers, detailed observation and rigorous documentation. Long interviews and participant observation were the core instruments over the two-week fieldwork. I also ran mapping exercises to understand the different demographics of the people living in the area.

## Who I worked with

The National Institute of Design field research team, for the Directorate of Tourism, Uttar Pradesh, and the residents, artisans and shopkeepers of Taj Ganj.
