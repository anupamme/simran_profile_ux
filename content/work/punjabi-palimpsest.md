## The question

How can a town in post-partition Punjab build a new relationship with a contested past, so that the Mughal heritage sites being vandalised are valued and protected by the community around them?

## My role

I worked as an ethnographer and interaction designer in a 3-person team, developing immersive narrations of the oral histories we gathered.

## Methods

The project combined participant observation, anthropological filmmaking, oral history and interactive design. An initial research phase was followed by skill-sharing, in which students from local colleges researched the heritage of their own town as a cultural palimpsest.

## Who I worked with

The project was guided by the University of Surrey and the British Council, in partnership with the Cultural Resource Conservation Initiative, which builds community-based heritage conservation from the ground up. We worked with students from local colleges, and with townspeople whose stories shaped the narrations.

## Impact

- The students presented their work at the Aam Khas Bagh, a 17th-century royal Mughal sarai. The public event was attended by **400 people**.
- Contributed to community-based heritage conservation, part of a wider effort to nominate the Grand Trunk Road as a UNESCO World Heritage site.
- Exhibited at the international conference "Interpretation of Built Heritage" in Chandigarh, and at the Alchemy festival, Southbank Centre, London (2013).
- Funded by the Arts & Humanities Research Council (2013). I also held a British Council Unbox fellowship (Project UnMap, 2013).
- **Paper:** Cultural Amnesia at the 'Aam Khaas Bagh', Society for Applied Anthropology 2016, Vancouver
