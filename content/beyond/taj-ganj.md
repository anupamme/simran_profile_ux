Research project for developing an integrated heritage-based development plan for Taj Ganj, Agra for the Directorate of Tourism, Uttar Pradesh, India. Taj Ganj, the ‘Heritage Precinct’, as defined by UNESCO. It is the area right outside the forecourt of Taj Mahal, that houses the the descendants of the artisans who built Taj Mahal and is home to cultural and built heritage, age old crafts, traditions and practices.

As part of a team of field researchers from National Institute of Design, the brief was to study the irregular and unbalanced flow of tourist traffic through the 3 entry gates to the Taj: East, West and South, that happen to lie right in the periphery of Taj Ganj. The idea was to create systems to regularize tourist traffic and to make inclusive experiences without disrupting the socio-cultural fabric of the place. My area of study was the residential area leading to the West Gate, one of the gates that attracts heavy tourist traffic.The process followed was the classic phenomenological approach of ethnography which included engaging with the community, building a rapport, gaining access to the site and its gatekeepers, detailed observations and rigorous documentation. Long Interviews and Participant Observations were the fundamental instruments used in this research during the two week period. I conducted mapping exercises to also understand the different demographics of the people living in the area.

Finally, data from the team members were collated into a report. Thereafter a master plan for modernization and building sustainable livelihoods, conservation of built heritage, protecting intangible cultural heritage assets and unique visitor experiences is now in the process of development and implementation.
